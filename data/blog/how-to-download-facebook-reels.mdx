Facebook doesn't offer a download option for Reels or regular videos on the web, whether you're viewing them in a browser or scrolling through the app. Saving one for offline use always needs a separate step.

## Steps

1. Open the Reel and copy its link — either from the address bar on desktop, or via the Share menu on mobile (Share → Copy Link).
2. Paste it into a [Facebook video downloader](/facebook-video-downloader).
3. Download the MP4 once the preview loads.

Regular Facebook videos (not just Reels) work the same way — the URL pattern is what a downloader uses to identify the content, not the video format itself.

## Why There's No Official Download Button

Like most social platforms, Facebook is built to keep video views inside the app and site rather than as downloadable files — that drives the ad and engagement metrics the platform is built around. This is a deliberate product decision across essentially every major video platform (Instagram, TikTok and YouTube all work the same way), not something specific to Facebook.

## Common Issues

**"This content isn't available."** Usually means the video is private, was posted in a private group, or was deleted. A browser-based downloader only has access to what Facebook makes publicly viewable — there's no way around a privacy restriction.

**Link doesn't work if copied from the mobile app.** Make sure the Share menu's "Copy Link" option was used rather than copying a shortened or app-internal link — those sometimes don't resolve the same way outside the app.

**Video downloads but title looks generic.** Facebook doesn't always expose a distinct title for every video the way YouTube does — some Reels and page videos only have a generic name available through public data, which a downloader can't invent.

## FAQ

**Does this work for Facebook Watch videos?**
Yes, as long as the video is public.

**Can I download videos from Facebook groups?**
Only if the group is public and the video doesn't require login to view.

**Is there a quality option, or just one download?**
This depends on what Facebook exposes for that specific video — some videos only have a single "Standard" quality available, which isn't a limitation of the downloader, just what Facebook's own hosting provides for that upload.
